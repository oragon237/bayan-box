# Bayan v3 Is Here: Traceable Earnings, Protected Commissions, and Smarter Delivery for Philippine Provinces

**Posted by the Bayan Team** · Product Update

> tl;dr — Bayan (formerly BayanBox) v3 brings six headline upgrades: per-role earnings traceability, a 72-hour affiliate commission hold that voids on cancellation, admin/staff financial settlement for COD cash, profile pages with fixed coordinates, merchant-origin delivery distance with a 100 km service-area cap, and a rider delivery map that shows the real store-to-door route.

Every peso moving through a provincial economy deserves a paper trail. That was the guiding idea behind Bayan v3 — a release focused on **trust, transparency, and fairness** for the six roles that make up our platform: customers, riders, merchants, providers, staff, and admins.

Here's everything new in v3.

## 1. Per-role earnings traceability 🧾

Gone are the days of wondering *"where did this commission come from?"* The affiliate earnings screen now breaks income down into **income sources** (marketplace orders, parcels, and micro-commissions) alongside a full double-entry **ledger history** showing description, amount, direction, and timestamp. Whether you're a customer, rider, or merchant, you can see exactly which commission came from which source — down to the individual transaction.

## 2. Affiliate commissions: held, then released — and protected ⏳

Affiliate commissions now pass through a **72-hour grace period** before they reach your wallet. During that hold, the amount sits safely in `pending_affiliate_commissions` and your dashboard shows it as "Pending (grace period)". If the order is cancelled, **the pending hold is voided** — the money simply never lands. This protects the program from referral abuse while keeping legitimate earners paid. Vested holds are released automatically on schedule into your affiliate wallet.

## 3. Financial settlement for admins and staff 💵

Cash on Delivery is a way of life in the provinces, and v3 finally gives admins and staff the tools to account for every peso. Admins get a **money-flow overview**: total collected by payment method, all wallet balances (including escrow), rider COD collections, merchant payables, and pending cash-outs. Staff get a **settlement console** to record rider COD remittances back to the hub — an audited trail of every batch of cash handed over.

## 4. Profiles with fixed coordinates 📍

Every role — customer, rider, provider, and merchant — now has an editable profile with **fixed latitude/longitude**, settable via a GPS button or manual entry. For merchants, that's a dedicated "Store location" card. Fixed coordinates are the foundation of accurate routing and delivery pricing across our service area.

## 5. Delivery measured from the merchant — capped at 100 km 🛵

Delivery distance is now calculated from **the merchant's store to the customer's delivery address**, not from some hub in the middle. That means fairer fees for short hops and honest pricing for long ones. And to protect everyone from runaway quotes, any delivery beyond our **100 km service-area cap** is politely rejected at checkout — with a clear out-of-range message — instead of billing you a surprise fee.

## 6. The rider merchant→customer delivery map 🗺️

Riders now see their deliveries on a real map: the **merchant (🏪) → customer (🏠)** route, with the store's name, location, barangay, and municipality attached to every order. A "Navigate" button opens turn-by-turn Google Maps directions straight from the store to the door. Less guesswork, fewer wrong turns, faster deliveries.
